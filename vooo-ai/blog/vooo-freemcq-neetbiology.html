Biology is the soul of the NEET UG examination. With a weightage of 360 marks out of the total 720 — exactly half the entire paper — Biology is the single most decisive subject in determining whether a NEET aspirant secures admission to a government medical college, a private medical institution, or misses the cutoff entirely. Unlike Physics and Chemistry, where a single conceptual gap can make entire question types inaccessible, Biology rewards consistent, thorough, and systematic study across a clearly defined NCERT-based syllabus. The students who score 340 or above in NEET Biology are almost invariably those who have treated the NCERT textbooks of Classes 11 and 12 as their primary scripture — reading every line, understanding every diagram, and practicing every concept through thousands of MCQs until recall becomes instantaneous and effortless.

Botany — the study of plant life — constitutes one of the two great pillars of NEET Biology and covers a syllabus of remarkable breadth and scientific depth. The diversity of plant life explored through the classification of Algae, Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms, and Angiosperms in the chapter on Plant Kingdom introduces students to the extraordinary evolutionary journey of plants from simple aquatic organisms to the complex flowering plants that dominate terrestrial ecosystems today. Morphology and Anatomy of Flowering Plants — covering root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, and seed structure along with the internal tissue organization revealed through transverse sections — is a chapter of high examination importance where diagram-based questions appear with remarkable consistency in every NEET paper.

Plant Physiology covers the dynamic biochemical and biophysical processes that sustain plant life — and it is a section that rewards deep conceptual understanding over superficial memorization. Transport in Plants, including the mechanisms of water absorption, ascent of sap, transpiration, and the pressure flow hypothesis of phloem transport, builds a mechanistic understanding of how materials move through the plant body. Mineral Nutrition explores the essential macro and micronutrients required by plants, the symptoms of their deficiency, and the fascinating process of biological nitrogen fixation. Photosynthesis in Higher Plants — covering the light reactions in the thylakoid membrane, the Calvin cycle in the stroma, C3 and C4 pathways, and the factors affecting photosynthesis — is one of the highest-weightage and most frequently tested topics in the entire NEET Biology paper.

Respiration in Plants and Plant Growth and Development complete the plant physiology section with concepts of equal importance and examination relevance. The detailed biochemistry of glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain — including the precise ATP yield at each stage — is a topic where NEET examiners consistently test candidates at the level of specific molecular details and numerical values. Plant Growth and Development introduces the concept of plant growth regulators — auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid, and ethylene — along with their discovery, chemical nature, physiological effects, and agricultural applications, all of which are favorite sources of NEET MCQ questions that test both factual recall and conceptual application.

Zoology — the study of animal life — brings the biological narrative from plants to the animal kingdom, including the human body, with equal depth and examination significance. The Animal Kingdom chapter, which classifies animals from Porifera through Chordata based on fundamental body organization principles, is a chapter of high factual density where specific characteristics, examples, and distinguishing features of each phylum and class must be memorized with precision. Structural Organisation in Animals — covering the tissue types, organ systems, and detailed anatomy of the earthworm, cockroach, and frog — is a chapter where diagram-based questions and questions on specific structural details appear with high frequency and reward candidates who have studied their NCERT diagrams with meticulous care.

Cell Biology and Cell Division represent the molecular and structural foundation of all biological understanding and carry enormous weightage in the NEET examination. The Cell — The Unit of Life covers the ultrastructure of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells in exceptional detail, with the structure and function of every organelle — nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplast, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, ribosomes, lysosomes, vacuoles, and the cell membrane — tested through both direct and application-based questions. Cell Division covers the precise stages of mitosis and meiosis with the specific chromosomal events, spindle behavior, and significance of each phase — a topic where the ability to identify stages from diagrams and to explain the significance of crossing over and independent assortment is consistently tested in NEET papers.

Genetics and Evolution is the conceptual crown of NEET Biology and the section that most powerfully separates the highest-scoring candidates from the rest. Principles of Inheritance and Variation — covering Mendel's laws, incomplete dominance, codominance, multiple alleles, polygenic inheritance, pleiotropy, sex-linked inheritance, and chromosomal disorders including Down syndrome, Turner syndrome, and Klinefelter syndrome — demands both thorough factual knowledge and the ability to solve genetics problems involving monohybrid, dihybrid, and test crosses. Molecular Basis of Inheritance — covering DNA structure, DNA replication, transcription, translation, the genetic code, gene regulation through the lac operon, and the Human Genome Project — is the single highest-weightage chapter in the entire NEET Biology syllabus and must be mastered with absolute thoroughness and precision.

Human Physiology spans a rich and medically relevant collection of chapters that together describe the functioning of the human body at the organ system level with a depth and detail that directly reflects the clinical knowledge base every future medical professional must possess. Digestion and Absorption, Breathing and Exchange of Gases, Body Fluids and Circulation, Excretory Products and their Elimination, Locomotion and Movement, Neural Control and Coordination, and Chemical Coordination and Integration — covering the endocrine system and its hormones — together constitute a syllabus of enormous examination importance. Questions from Human Physiology chapters collectively account for a substantial proportion of the NEET Biology paper every year, and candidates who master the specific numerical values, anatomical details, and physiological mechanisms in these chapters consistently achieve the high Biology scores that make the difference between selection and rejection in India's most competitive medical entrance examination. 1The cell theory was proposed by:
A. Robert Hooke
B. Schleiden and Schwann
C. Louis Pasteur
D. Rudolf Virchow
Answer: B - Schleiden and Schwann
Cell theory was proposed by Matthias Schleiden (1838, for plants) and Theodor Schwann (1839, for animals). The theory states: (1) All living organisms are made of cells; (2) The cell is the basic unit of life. Rudolf Virchow later added the third principle: (3) All cells arise from pre-existing cells (Omnis cellula e cellula).
2The site of ATP synthesis during cellular respiration is:
A. Cytoplasm
B. Nucleus
C. Inner mitochondrial membrane
D. Ribosome
Answer: C - Inner mitochondrial membrane
ATP synthesis during oxidative phosphorylation occurs on the inner mitochondrial membrane via ATP synthase (Complex V). The electron transport chain creates a proton gradient across the inner membrane; protons flow back through ATP synthase, driving ATP synthesis. This is called chemiosmosis, proposed by Peter Mitchell (Nobel Prize 1978).
3Which of the following is not a connective tissue?
A. Blood
B. Bone
C. Cartilage
D. Skin epithelium
Answer: D - Skin epithelium
Connective tissues include blood, bone, cartilage, adipose tissue, areolar tissue and lymph. Epithelial tissue (skin, gut lining) is NOT connective tissue - it covers body surfaces and lines cavities. The four basic tissue types in animals are: epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous tissues.
4DNA replication is called semi-conservative because:
A. New DNA has one old and one new strand
B. Only half the DNA is replicated
C. Replication occurs in half the cells
D. Half the bases are conserved
Answer: A - New DNA has one old and one new strand
Semi-conservative replication (proven by Meselson and Stahl, 1958) means each new DNA double helix consists of one original (parental) strand and one newly synthesised strand. The two strands of the parent DNA separate, and each serves as a template for a new complementary strand.
5Pollination by wind is called:
A. Entomophily
B. Ornithophily
C. Anemophily
D. Hydrophily
Answer: C - Anemophily
Anemophily is pollination by wind. Wind-pollinated flowers (e.g., grasses, maize) produce light, smooth, non-sticky pollen in large quantities. Entomophily = by insects, Ornithophily = by birds, Hydrophily = by water, Zoophily = by animals. Most flowering plants are insect-pollinated.
6The genetic material in most viruses is:
A. Only DNA
B. Only RNA
C. DNA or RNA, not both
D. Both DNA and RNA
Answer: C - DNA or RNA, not both
Viruses contain either DNA or RNA as their genetic material, but never both simultaneously. DNA viruses: Herpesvirus, Adenovirus, Poxvirus. RNA viruses: HIV, Influenza, Coronavirus, Poliovirus. This contrasts with cells, which always have both DNA and RNA. Viroids contain only RNA; prions contain only protein.
7Which hormone is called the emergency hormone?
A. Insulin
B. Thyroxine
C. Adrenaline (Epinephrine)
D. Glucagon
Answer: C - Adrenaline (Epinephrine)
Adrenaline (epinephrine), secreted by the adrenal medulla, is called the emergency hormone or "fight or flight" hormone. It is released rapidly in response to stress, fear or exercise. It increases heart rate, blood pressure, blood glucose and prepares the body for immediate action. It is also used medically for anaphylaxis.
8The process of formation of gametes is called:
A. Mitosis
B. Meiosis
C. Gametogenesis
D. Fertilisation
Answer: C - Gametogenesis
Gametogenesis is the process of formation of gametes (sex cells - sperm and eggs) through meiosis. Spermatogenesis produces sperm in the testes; oogenesis produces eggs in the ovaries. Meiosis reduces the chromosome number by half (haploid gametes), so that fertilisation restores the diploid number.
9Which part of the human brain regulates body temperature?
A. Cerebrum
B. Cerebellum
C. Hypothalamus
D. Medulla oblongata
Answer: C - Hypothalamus
The hypothalamus is the body's thermostat, regulating temperature by controlling sweating, shivering and blood vessel dilation. It also controls hunger, thirst, sleep, emotions and the pituitary gland (master gland). It links the nervous system to the endocrine system through the pituitary gland.
10Biodiversity hotspots in India include:
A. Thar Desert
B. Indo-Gangetic Plain
C. Western Ghats and Eastern Himalayas
D. Deccan Plateau
Answer: C - Western Ghats and Eastern Himalayas
India has four biodiversity hotspots: Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Eastern Himalayas (Indo-Burma), Sundaland, and Western Ghats. Biodiversity hotspots are regions with exceptional concentrations of endemic species that have lost at least 70% of their original habitat. Globally there are 36 biodiversity hotspots.
