The SBI PO examination is widely regarded as the most prestigious and competitive banking recruitment examination in India. Selection as a Probationary Officer in the State Bank of India — the country's largest public sector bank with thousands of branches across every state and union territory and a balance sheet that dwarfs every other financial institution in the nation — represents one of the most coveted career achievements available to any banking aspirant. The SBI PO examination is designed with deliberate rigor to identify candidates of exceptional intellectual caliber, analytical depth, and general awareness who are capable of growing into the leadership roles that India's premier banking institution demands from its officers.

Banking and Financial Awareness forms the most directly relevant and consistently high-weightage component of the SBI PO General Awareness section. A thorough understanding of the structure and functioning of the Indian banking system — including the role of the Reserve Bank of India as the central bank and regulator, the classification of scheduled and non-scheduled banks, the functioning of public sector and private sector banks, the distinction between commercial banks and cooperative banks, and the role of development finance institutions — is absolutely essential for every serious SBI PO aspirant. Monetary policy instruments including the repo rate, reverse repo rate, Cash Reserve Ratio, Statutory Liquidity Ratio, Marginal Standing Facility, and Open Market Operations are among the most frequently tested topics and require not just memorization of current values but genuine conceptual understanding of how these tools influence money supply, credit availability, and inflation in the broader economy.

The history and evolution of banking in India — from the establishment of the Bank of Hindustan in 1770 through the nationalization of fourteen major commercial banks in 1969 and six more in 1980, to the liberalization of the banking sector in the 1990s and the more recent introduction of Small Finance Banks and Payment Banks — provides essential historical context that enriches a candidate's understanding of the current banking landscape. SBI's own history, its merger with the five associate banks and Bharatiya Mahila Bank in 2017, its organizational structure, its flagship products and services, and its role in implementing government financial inclusion schemes are topics that carry particular importance in the SBI PO examination and reflect the recruiter's expectation that successful candidates will enter the organization with genuine institutional knowledge and pride.

Static General Knowledge covers a broad and diverse range of topics that together test the depth and breadth of a candidate's general awareness. Important international organizations and their headquarters, India's national symbols, important days and years, books and authors, awards and honors including the Bharat Ratna, Padma awards, Nobel Prizes, and national sports awards, famous personalities and their contributions, important treaties and agreements, and the capitals and currencies of world nations — all form part of the static GK landscape that SBI PO aspirants must cover comprehensively. While individual static GK questions may appear straightforward, the sheer breadth of the topic means that only candidates who have prepared systematically and practiced regularly can consistently score well across this section.

Indian Economy is a section of profound importance for the SBI PO examination, reflecting the deep connection between banking, finance, and the broader economic environment in which the State Bank of India operates. Understanding India's GDP composition, the structure of the Union Budget, fiscal deficit and its implications, the functioning of SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, and NABARD alongside the RBI, the priority sector lending framework, financial inclusion initiatives including Jan Dhan Yojana, Mudra loans, and the PM SVANidhi scheme, and India's external sector including balance of payments, foreign exchange reserves, and trade policy — all of these topics appear with high frequency in SBI PO Mains papers and reward candidates who have studied economics not just as an examination subject but as a genuine framework for understanding how the national and global economy functions.

Economic and Social Issues bring a policy-oriented and development-focused dimension to the SBI PO General Awareness syllabus that distinguishes it from purely banking-focused examinations. Topics such as poverty and inequality, unemployment and employment generation schemes, agricultural distress and rural development, infrastructure investment, urbanization and smart cities, digital economy and fintech innovation, gender equity and women's empowerment, health policy and the National Health Mission, and education reform are all areas where candidates are expected to demonstrate not just factual knowledge but informed analytical awareness. The SBI PO Mains examination in particular tests the ability to connect these social and economic issues to the banking sector's role in driving inclusive development — reflecting the State Bank of India's self-understanding as an institution with a social mission that goes beyond profit generation.

Current Affairs with a specific focus on banking, finance, economy, and national development is the dynamic layer that ties the entire SBI PO General Awareness syllabus together and gives the examination its contemporary relevance and vitality. Recent RBI policy decisions, new banking regulations and compliance requirements, major mergers and acquisitions in the financial sector, landmark government schemes and their implementation status, India's performance in major international indices, significant appointments in the banking and financial sector, and important developments in the global economy — all of these find their way into SBI PO examination papers with reliable frequency. The margin between selection and rejection in the SBI PO examination is consistently narrow, and the advantage belongs to those candidates who combine deep static knowledge with sharp, up-to-date current affairs awareness and who practice consistently and intelligently every single day. 🏦 SBI PO General Awareness
1SBI was established in which year?
A. 1947
B. 1950
C. 1955
D. 1969
Answer: C — 1955
State Bank of India was established on 1 July 1955 when the Imperial Bank of India was nationalised and renamed. The Imperial Bank was itself formed in 1921 from the merger of three presidency banks. SBI is India's largest commercial bank and a Fortune 500 company.
2The headquarters of SBI is located in:
A. New Delhi
B. Mumbai
C. Kolkata
D. Chennai
Answer: B — Mumbai
The corporate headquarters of State Bank of India is located at State Bank Bhavan, Madame Cama Road, Mumbai (Maharashtra). SBI has over 22,000 branches and 65,000 ATMs across India, making it the largest bank branch network in the country.
3PMJDY stands for:
A. Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana
B. Pradhan Mantri Jan Dharm Yojana
C. Prime Minister Jan Dhan Yojana
D. Pradhan Mantri Jal Dhara Yojana
Answer: A — Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana
PMJDY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana) is India's national financial inclusion mission launched on 28 August 2014. It aims to ensure affordable access to financial services for all households. Account holders get a RuPay debit card and life insurance cover. It is the world's largest financial inclusion initiative.
4The full form of NEFT is:
A. National Electronic Funds Transfer
B. National Efficient Funds Transfer
C. National Electronic Finance Transfer
D. Net Electronic Funds Transfer
Answer: A — National Electronic Funds Transfer
NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer) is a nationwide payment system operated by RBI that facilitates one-to-one transfer of funds between bank accounts. NEFT operates on a deferred net settlement basis in hourly batches. Since December 2019, NEFT operates 24x7 including weekends and holidays.
5Which committee recommended the merger of associate banks with SBI?
A. Narasimham Committee
B. Nachiket Mor Committee
C. SBI Board itself
D. RBI Working Group
Answer: A — Narasimham Committee
The Narasimham Committee on banking sector reforms recommended consolidation of public sector banks. Following these recommendations and to create a stronger global bank, SBI merged its five associate banks (State Bank of Bikaner and Jaipur, State Bank of Hyderabad etc.) and Bharatiya Mahila Bank with itself in 2017.
6RTGS is used for:
A. Small value transactions
B. Large value transactions in real time
C. International transfers only
D. Mobile payments only
Answer: B — Large value transactions in real time
RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement) is used for large-value fund transfers (minimum ₹2 lakh) that are settled in real time — meaning transactions are processed immediately and individually. Unlike NEFT which is batch-based, RTGS settles each transaction individually. It operates 24x7 since December 2020.
7The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) was enacted in:
A. 2014
B. 2015
C. 2016
D. 2018
Answer: C — 2016
The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) was enacted in 2016 to consolidate and amend laws relating to reorganisation and insolvency resolution of corporate persons, partnership firms and individuals. It created the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) as the adjudicating authority for corporate insolvencies.
8Priority Sector Lending target for domestic commercial banks is:
A. 30% of ANBC
B. 35% of ANBC
C. 40% of ANBC
D. 45% of ANBC
Answer: C — 40% of ANBC
RBI mandates domestic commercial banks to lend 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) to priority sectors including agriculture (18%), micro enterprises, education, housing and weaker sections. This ensures credit flows to sectors that may not otherwise have adequate access to bank credit.
9Which payment system was launched by NPCI for small transactions?
A. NEFT
B. RTGS
C. UPI
D. IMPS
Answer: C — UPI
UPI (Unified Payments Interface) was launched by NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India) in 2016. It allows instant money transfers between bank accounts using a mobile number or UPI ID (VPA). UPI has become the most popular digital payment method in India, processing billions of transactions monthly.
10Basel III norms relate to:
A. Insurance regulation
B. Bank capital adequacy and risk
C. Stock market regulation
D. Foreign exchange regulation
Answer: B — Bank capital adequacy and risk
Basel III is a global regulatory framework developed by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision to strengthen bank capital requirements and introduce new regulatory requirements on bank liquidity and leverage. It requires banks to maintain higher quality capital and liquidity buffers to withstand financial stress.
