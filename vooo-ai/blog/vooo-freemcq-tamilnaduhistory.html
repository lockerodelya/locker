🏛️ Tamil Nadu History & Culture
1The capital of Tamil Nadu is:
A. Madurai
B. Coimbatore
C. Chennai
D. Trichy
Answer: C — Chennai
Chennai (formerly Madras) is the capital of Tamil Nadu and the fourth largest city in India. It is known as the Detroit of India for its automobile industry and the Gateway to South India. Chennai was founded by the British East India Company in 1639 when Francis Day obtained land from the Nayak ruler.
2The Chola Empire was famous for:
A. Mauryan administration
B. Naval power and trade
C. Mughal architecture
D. Vedic rituals
Answer: B — Naval power and trade
The medieval Chola Empire (9th-13th centuries CE) was famous for its powerful navy, overseas trade and temple architecture. Chola kings like Rajaraja I and Rajendra I built magnificent temples and launched naval expeditions to Southeast Asia, spreading Tamil culture and trade across the Indian Ocean.
3The Brihadeeswarar Temple in Thanjavur was built by:
A. Rajendra Chola
B. Rajaraja Chola I
C. Kulottunga Chola
D. Vikramaditya
Answer: B — Rajaraja Chola I
The Brihadeeswarar Temple (Big Temple) in Thanjavur was built by Chola Emperor Rajaraja I and completed in 1010 CE. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a masterpiece of Dravidian architecture. The temple's vimana (tower) is 66 metres tall and was the tallest temple tower in India at the time of its construction.
4The oldest classical language of India is:
A. Sanskrit
B. Tamil
C. Telugu
D. Kannada
Answer: B — Tamil
Tamil is considered the oldest living classical language in the world, with a literary tradition spanning over 2,000 years. The Sangam literature (300 BCE - 300 CE) is the oldest body of secular literature in any language. Tamil was recognised as a Classical Language of India in 2004.
5Periyar E.V. Ramasamy is associated with which movement?
A. Non-Cooperation Movement
B. Self-Respect Movement
C. Khilafat Movement
D. Chipko Movement
Answer: B — Self-Respect Movement
Periyar E.V. Ramasamy (1879-1973) founded the Self-Respect Movement in Tamil Nadu in 1925. It fought against Brahminical dominance, caste discrimination and superstitions. He also founded the Dravidar Kazhagam party. He is called Thanthai Periyar and the Father of the Dravidian Movement.
6The Meenakshi Amman Temple is located in:
A. Chennai
B. Kanchipuram
C. Madurai
D. Thanjavur
Answer: C — Madurai
The Meenakshi Amman Temple is located in Madurai, one of the oldest cities in the world. The temple is dedicated to Goddess Meenakshi (a form of Parvati) and Lord Sundareswarar (Shiva). It has 14 towering gopurams (gateway towers) decorated with thousands of colourful sculptures.
7Bharatanatyam is a classical dance form originating from:
A. Kerala
B. Tamil Nadu
C. Andhra Pradesh
D. Karnataka
Answer: B — Tamil Nadu
Bharatanatyam is one of the oldest classical dance forms of India, originating from Tamil Nadu. It evolved from the Sadir dance performed by Devadasis in temples. The dance was revived and popularised in the 20th century by Rukmini Devi Arundale. It is characterised by fixed upper torso, bent legs and intricate footwork.
8Kanchipuram in Tamil Nadu is famous for:
A. Diamonds
B. Silk sarees
C. Automobiles
D. Software exports
Answer: B — Silk sarees
Kanchipuram is world-famous for its handwoven pure silk sarees (Kanjivaram sarees), known for their distinctive patterns, rich colours and durability. The city is also called the City of Thousand Temples and is one of the seven sacred cities in Hinduism. It was the capital of the Pallava kingdom.
9The Nilgiri Hills in Tamil Nadu are also called:
A. Blue Mountains
B. Green Mountains
C. Silver Mountains
D. Golden Mountains
Answer: A — Blue Mountains
The Nilgiri Hills (Nilgiris means Blue Mountains in Tamil) get their name from the blue flowers of the Neelakurinji plant that blooms once every 12 years, covering the hills in blue. Ooty (Udhagamandalam) is the main hill station in the Nilgiris, known as the Queen of Hill Stations.
10C.V. Raman, who won the Nobel Prize in Physics, was from:
A. Mumbai
B. Chennai
C. Trichy
D. Coimbatore
Answer: B — Chennai
Sir C.V. Raman was born in Tiruchirapalli (Trichy), Tamil Nadu in 1888 and worked at the Indian Association for the Cultivation of Science in Calcutta. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930 for discovering the Raman Effect (scattering of light). 28 February is celebrated as National Science Day in his honour.
