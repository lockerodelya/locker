Assam Geography and Culture MCQ Practice

Essential questions covering Brahmaputra, Tea Gardens, Kaziranga and Bihu Culture

These essential MCQ questions are selected from the most important topics in Assam state examinations. Sections include Assam Geography, Brahmaputra River, Tea Industry, Kaziranga National Park, Bihu Culture, Ahom Kingdom, Silk Industry and Current Affairs of Assam — important for APSC, Assam Police, SLRC and other state-level competitive examinations. Assam is one of the most geographically and culturally diverse states of India, and a deep understanding of its land, rivers, forests, people and history is absolutely essential for anyone preparing for Assam state competitive examinations. The Brahmaputra River, one of the mightiest rivers in the world, flows through the entire length of Assam from east to west and shapes the state's geography, economy, flood patterns and biodiversity in profound ways. Questions on the Brahmaputra, its tributaries like the Subansiri, Manas, Beki and Dhansiri, and the recurring floods of Assam are regularly tested in APSC and Assam Police examinations. Assam is the largest tea-producing state in India, and the famous Assam tea gardens of Dibrugarh, Jorhat, Tinsukia and Sivasagar districts are a critical topic for state-level MCQ practice. The history of tea cultivation in Assam, its role in the Indian and global economy, and the social history of tea garden workers are all important examination subjects. Kaziranga National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site located in Golaghat and Nagaon districts, is home to the world's largest population of the Indian one-horned rhinoceros and is one of the most frequently tested topics in Assam geography MCQs. Manas National Park, another UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the Dibru-Saikhowa National Park are also important wildlife and geography topics. The Ahom Kingdom, which ruled Assam for nearly 600 years from 1228 to 1826 and successfully resisted Mughal invasions seventeen times, is a major subject in Assam history MCQs. The Battle of Saraighat in 1671, where Lachit Borphukan led the Ahom forces to a decisive naval victory against the Mughals, is one of the most important historical events tested in APSC examinations. Assam's silk industry, particularly Muga silk which is unique to Assam and recognized with a Geographical Indication tag, along with Pat silk and Eri silk, forms an important part of the state's cultural and economic identity and appears frequently in competitive exam questions. Bihu, the most celebrated festival of Assam celebrated three times a year as Rongali Bihu, Kongali Bihu and Bhogali Bihu, reflects the agricultural and cultural life of the Assamese people and is a key topic in Assam culture MCQs. The Sattriya dance, recognized as one of the eight classical dance forms of India and originating from the Vaishnavite monasteries called Sattras of Assam, is another important cultural subject. The geography of Assam also includes the Barak Valley in the south, the hills of Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao, and the vast floodplains of the Brahmaputra Valley, all of which are tested in state examinations. 1The capital of Assam is:
A. Jorhat
B. Silchar
C. Guwahati
D. Dispur
Answer: D — Dispur
Dispur (a locality within Guwahati) is the capital of Assam. Guwahati, situated on the banks of the Brahmaputra, is the largest city in Northeast India and the gateway to the Northeast. The Kamakhya Temple on Nilachal Hill is one of the most revered Shakti Pithas in India.
2The Brahmaputra River enters Assam from:
A. Bangladesh
B. China (Tibet)
C. Arunachal Pradesh
D. Bhutan
Answer: C — Arunachal Pradesh
The Brahmaputra River enters India through Arunachal Pradesh (as the Siang River) after flowing through Tibet (as the Tsangpo). It enters Assam near Sadiya and flows west for about 700 km through the Assam Valley before entering Bangladesh. In Assam it is called Luit or Burhi Luit. It is one of the world's largest rivers by discharge.
3Kaziranga National Park in Assam is famous for:
A. Bengal Tigers
B. One-horned Rhinoceros
C. Asiatic Lions
D. Snow Leopards
Answer: B — One-horned Rhinoceros
Kaziranga National Park in Golaghat and Nagaon districts is a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for hosting two-thirds of the world's population of the Indian one-horned rhinoceros. It also has the world's highest density of tigers. Kaziranga hosts significant populations of elephants, wild buffaloes and swamp deer.
4Bihu is a festival of Assam celebrated to mark:
A. Religious events
B. Three seasons of the agricultural year
C. Victory in battles
D. Royal coronations
Answer: B — Three seasons of the agricultural year
Bihu is Assam's most important festival, celebrated three times a year: Bohag Bihu (spring/Assamese New Year, April), Magh Bihu (harvest festival, January) and Kati Bihu (autumn, October). Bohag Bihu is the most important and is associated with the Bihu dance, a vibrant folk dance of Assam.
5Assam produces about what percentage of India's total tea?
A. 20%
B. 35%
C. 52%
D. 70%
Answer: C — 52%
Assam produces about 52% of India's total tea output, making it the world's largest tea-growing region. Assam tea is known for its strong, malty and robust flavour. The Brahmaputra valley's warm, moist climate, frequent rainfall and unique soil are ideal for tea cultivation. Assam has over 800 large tea estates.
6The Ahom Kingdom ruled Assam for approximately:
A. 100 years
B. 300 years
C. 600 years
D. 1000 years
Answer: C — 600 years
The Ahom Kingdom ruled Assam for about 600 years (1228-1826 CE), making it one of the longest-ruling dynasties in Indian history. The Ahoms, who migrated from present-day Myanmar/Thailand, successfully resisted 17 Mughal invasions. The kingdom finally fell to the Burmese and was later annexed by the British.
7Muga silk, a golden-coloured silk unique to Assam, is produced by:
A. Silkworms fed on mulberry
B. Wild silkworms on som and sualu trees
C. Silkworms fed on oak leaves
D. Domesticated silkworms on cotton
Answer: B — Wild silkworms on som and sualu trees
Muga silk is a variety of wild silk unique to Assam, produced by the Antheraea assamensis silkworm that feeds on som and sualu trees. It has a natural golden-yellow sheen that intensifies with each wash. Muga silk has a GI (Geographical Indication) tag and is one of the most durable natural silks in the world.
8The Majuli River Island in Assam is the world's:
A. Largest river island
B. Largest freshwater island
C. Oldest inhabited island
D. Most biodiverse island
Answer: A — Largest river island
Majuli in the Brahmaputra River is the world's largest river island (though it has shrunk due to erosion). It is the centre of the Vaishnavite neo-monastic movement started by the 15th-century saint Srimanta Shankardev. The island has numerous satras (Vaishnavite monasteries) and is a UNESCO tentative World Heritage Site.
9Which is the state animal of Assam?
A. Tiger
B. Elephant
C. One-horned Rhinoceros
D. Wild Buffalo
Answer: C — One-horned Rhinoceros
The Indian one-horned rhinoceros (Rhinoceros unicornis) is the state animal of Assam. Assam's Kaziranga National Park protects the largest population of this endangered species. The state bird is the white-winged wood duck and the state flower is the Foxtail Orchid (Rhynchostylis retusa).
10The Bodo tribe is the largest plains tribal community in Assam. Their traditional dance is:
A. Bihu
B. Bagurumba
C. Sattriya
D. Ojapali
Answer: B — Bagurumba
Bagurumba is the traditional dance of the Bodo tribe of Assam, often called the butterfly dance for its graceful movements that mimic butterflies. The Bodos are the largest plains tribal community in Assam and Northeast India. Their traditional attire includes the dokhna (women) and gamcha (men). Bodoland Territorial Council governs their region.
