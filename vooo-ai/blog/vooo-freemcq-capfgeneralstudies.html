CAPF General Studies

CAPF General Studies MCQ Practice

Essential questions covering History, Geography, Polity, Science and Defence

These essential MCQ questions are selected from the most important topics in CAPF (Central Armed Police Forces) AC (Assistant Commandant) examinations conducted by UPSC. Sections include General Studies, General Science, History, Indian Polity, Geography and Current Affairs for BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP and SSB.

The CAPF AC examination conducted by the Union Public Service Commission is one of the most prestigious and competitive examinations in India for candidates aspiring to serve as officers in the Central Armed Police Forces. The examination selects Assistant Commandants for five major forces — the Border Security Force, the Central Reserve Police Force, the Central Industrial Security Force, the Indo-Tibetan Border Police, and the Sashastra Seema Bal — each of which plays a vital and distinct role in safeguarding India's borders, maintaining internal security, protecting critical infrastructure, and ensuring national integrity. The examination demands not only physical fitness and mental toughness but also a deep and well-rounded command of General Studies that spans history, geography, polity, science, and current affairs.

General Studies forms the broadest and most comprehensive section of the CAPF AC examination, and it requires aspirants to build a wide base of knowledge that cuts across multiple disciplines. Questions from this section test a candidate's awareness of India and the world across historical, geographical, political, economic, scientific, and cultural dimensions. The ability to connect facts across subjects, draw informed conclusions, and apply general awareness to real-world scenarios is what separates top-performing candidates from the rest. A systematic and topic-wise approach to General Studies, supported by regular MCQ practice, is the most effective way to master this vast and dynamic section.

History questions in the CAPF AC examination cover Ancient, Medieval, and Modern Indian History as well as key aspects of World History. Ancient Indian history topics include the Indus Valley Civilisation, the Vedic Age, the Maurya and Gupta empires, the spread of Buddhism and Jainism, and the cultural and scientific achievements of early India. Medieval history covers the Delhi Sultanate, the Mughal Empire, the Vijayanagara Kingdom, the Maratha Empire, and the Bhakti and Sufi movements that shaped Indian society and religion. Modern Indian history places particular emphasis on the colonial period, the various phases of the freedom struggle, the contributions of national leaders such as Mahatma Gandhi, Subhas Chandra Bose, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, and Bhagat Singh, and the political developments that led to India's independence and partition in 1947. World history topics include the French Revolution, the Industrial Revolution, the two World Wars, the Cold War, and the emergence of the post-colonial world order.

Indian Polity is one of the most scoring and consistently tested sections of the CAPF AC examination, and it demands a thorough understanding of the Constitution of India, its fundamental features, and the institutions and processes it establishes. Topics such as the Preamble, Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles of State Policy, Fundamental Duties, the structure and powers of Parliament, the President and the Executive, the Judiciary and the Supreme Court, the federal structure and Centre-State relations, the amendment procedure, and important constitutional bodies such as the Election Commission, the UPSC, and the CAG are all regularly examined. Aspirants must also be familiar with landmark constitutional amendments, important Supreme Court judgments, and recent developments in Indian governance and public administration that reflect the evolving interpretation of the Constitution.

Geography questions in the CAPF AC examination span both physical and human geography of India and the world. Physical geography topics include the structure of the Earth, climate systems, ocean currents, monsoon patterns, mountain ranges, river systems, soil types, and natural vegetation. Indian geography covers the Himalayan ranges, the Indo-Gangetic Plain, the Deccan Plateau, the Western and Eastern Ghats, the major rivers of India and their tributaries, the distribution of rainfall, the agricultural zones, and the mineral and energy resources of the country. Human geography topics include population distribution, urbanisation, transport and communication networks, economic geography, and India's geopolitical position in South Asia and the broader Indo-Pacific region. A strong command of maps, physical features, and the ability to relate geographical factors to historical and contemporary events is a significant advantage in this section.

General Science covers Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and Environmental Science at a level that tests both conceptual understanding and applied knowledge. Physics topics include laws of motion, gravitation, thermodynamics, electricity and magnetism, optics, and modern physics including nuclear energy and space science. Chemistry questions cover atomic structure, chemical bonding, acids, bases and salts, metals and non-metals, carbon compounds, and everyday chemistry related to food, medicine, and materials. Biology topics span cell biology, genetics, human physiology, plant science, ecology, and disease and nutrition, with particular attention to topics that have direct relevance to public health and the environment. Environmental Science questions cover climate change, biodiversity, pollution, conservation policies, and India's commitments under international environmental agreements, all of which are increasingly important in the context of national security and border management.

Current Affairs is arguably the most dynamic and challenging section of the CAPF AC examination, requiring aspirants to stay consistently updated with national and international developments across politics, economy, science and technology, defence, diplomacy, sports, and culture. Defence and security current affairs are of particular importance given the nature of the CAPF AC role, and candidates must be well-informed about developments along India's borders, new defence acquisitions and technologies, changes in the leadership and structure of the armed forces and paramilitary organisations, and India's bilateral and multilateral security engagements. Economic current affairs including Union Budget highlights, major government schemes, GDP and inflation data, and flagship programmes such as Make in India, Digital India, and Atmanirbhar Bharat are also regularly tested.

The Physical Standard and Medical examination along with the Personal Interview round make the CAPF AC selection process truly holistic, testing not just academic knowledge but also leadership potential, communication skills, and the personal qualities required of an officer who will command personnel in some of the most challenging and sensitive postings in the country. However, success in the written examination, which includes both the General Studies MCQ paper and the General Ability and Intelligence paper, is the critical first step that determines whether a candidate even reaches the physical and interview stages. Rigorous and consistent MCQ practice across all General Studies topics is therefore the most important investment a CAPF AC aspirant can make in their preparation journey.

1CAPF stands for:
A. Central Armed Police Forces
B. Central Army Police Forces
C. Combined Armed Police Forces
D. Central Auxiliary Police Forces
Answer: A — Central Armed Police Forces
CAPF (Central Armed Police Forces) includes BSF (Border Security Force), CRPF (Central Reserve Police Force), CISF (Central Industrial Security Force), ITBP (Indo-Tibetan Border Police) and SSB (Sashastra Seema Bal). UPSC conducts the AC (Assistant Commandant) recruitment exam.
2The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in:
A. Part II
B. Part III
C. Part IV
D. Part V
Answer: C — Part IV
The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are enshrined in Part IV (Articles 36-51) of the Indian Constitution. They are non-justiciable — courts cannot enforce them. However, they are fundamental to governance and the state must apply them in making laws. They are borrowed from the Irish Constitution.
3The Brahmos missile is a joint venture between India and:
A. USA
B. Russia
C. France
D. Israel
Answer: B — Russia
BrahMos is a supersonic cruise missile developed jointly by India's DRDO and Russia's NPO Mashinostroyeniya. The name BrahMos is derived from the Brahmaputra (India) and Moskva (Russia) rivers. It is one of the fastest supersonic cruise missiles in the world, flying at Mach 2.8.
4Which movement was associated with the slogan "Jai Jawan Jai Kisan"?
A. Non-Cooperation Movement
B. Chipko Movement
C. 1965 Indo-Pak War
D. Green Revolution
Answer: C — 1965 Indo-Pak War
"Jai Jawan Jai Kisan" (Hail the Soldier, Hail the Farmer) was the famous slogan given by Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri during the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965. It honoured both the soldier defending the nation and the farmer feeding it. Later, PM Vajpayee added "Jai Vigyan" (Hail Science).
5The Indian Ocean Tsunami of 2004 was caused by:
A. Volcanic eruption
B. Undersea earthquake
C. Cyclone
D. Meteor impact
Answer: B — Undersea earthquake
The 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami was triggered by a massive undersea earthquake of magnitude 9.1-9.3 off the coast of Sumatra, Indonesia on 26 December 2004. It is the deadliest tsunami in recorded history, killing over 2,27,000 people across 14 countries including India.
6Which Article of the Constitution can be used to impose President's Rule?
A. Article 352
B. Article 356
C. Article 360
D. Article 365
Answer: B — Article 356
Article 356 allows the President to impose President's Rule (State Emergency) in a state if the constitutional machinery of that state has failed. The state government is dismissed and the Governor administers the state on behalf of the President. It must be approved by Parliament.
7Operation Blue Star (1984) was conducted at:
A. Amritsar
B. Chandigarh
C. Ludhiana
D. Jalandhar
Answer: A — Amritsar
Operation Blue Star was a military operation conducted by the Indian Army from 1-8 June 1984 at the Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) complex in Amritsar, Punjab, under orders from PM Indira Gandhi. Its aim was to remove Sikh militants who had fortified the complex.
8Which gas causes the greenhouse effect most significantly?
A. Oxygen
B. Nitrogen
C. Carbon dioxide
D. Argon
Answer: C — Carbon dioxide
Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is the most significant greenhouse gas contributing to global warming and climate change. Others include methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O) and water vapour. The greenhouse effect traps heat in Earth's atmosphere, causing global temperatures to rise.
9The Durand Line is the border between:
A. India and Pakistan
B. India and China
C. Pakistan and Afghanistan
D. Afghanistan and Iran
Answer: C — Pakistan and Afghanistan
The Durand Line is the 2,640 km border between Pakistan and Afghanistan, established in 1893 during British India. It was drawn by Sir Mortimer Durand. Afghanistan has historically disputed this border. Many Pashtun tribes are divided by this line.
10Which Schedule of the Constitution lists the official languages of India?
A. Sixth Schedule
B. Seventh Schedule
C. Eighth Schedule
D. Ninth Schedule
Answer: C — Eighth Schedule
The Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution lists the 22 officially recognised languages of India. Originally there were 14 languages; Sindhi was added in 1967 (21st Amendment), then Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali in 1992 (71st Amendment), and Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santhali in 2003 (92nd Amendment).
