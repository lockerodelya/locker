Physics in the UPSC General Science section covers a wide range of fundamental and applied topics that are regularly tested in Civil Services Prelims. Laws of motion, gravitation, work energy and power, properties of matter, thermodynamics, waves and sound, light and optics, electricity and magnetism, nuclear physics and the principles underlying modern technological devices are all critical physics topics for UPSC MCQ preparation. Questions on the applications of physics in everyday life, such as the working of transformers, electric motors, sonar systems, optical fibres and nuclear reactors, are particularly favoured by UPSC question setters and appear with great regularity in previous year papers. Chemistry for UPSC General Science covers both theoretical concepts and their practical applications in everyday life and industry. Important topics include the periodic table and periodicity of properties, chemical bonding, acids bases and salts, oxidation and reduction reactions, electrochemistry, polymers and plastics, fuels and combustion, fertilisers and pesticides, food adulteration and food preservation, and the chemistry of common substances encountered in daily life. Environmental chemistry topics such as air pollution, water pollution, acid rain, the ozone layer, greenhouse gases and climate change are critically important areas in UPSC General Science as they directly connect chemistry with environment and ecology, another major section of the Prelims paper. Biology is arguably the most heavily weighted component of UPSC General Science and covers an extraordinarily wide range of topics from cell biology and genetics to human physiology, ecology and modern biotechnology. Cell structure and function, cell division, DNA replication and protein synthesis, Mendelian genetics and modern genetics, evolution and natural selection, classification of living organisms, plant physiology including photosynthesis and respiration, and the human organ systems including the digestive, respiratory, circulatory, excretory, nervous and endocrine systems are all fundamental biology topics that every UPSC aspirant must master thoroughly. Diseases and their causative agents, modes of transmission, prevention and treatment, including bacterial diseases like tuberculosis and cholera, viral diseases like dengue, COVID-19 and influenza, parasitic diseases like malaria and filariasis, and deficiency diseases caused by lack of vitamins and minerals, are perennially important topics in UPSC General Science MCQs. Biotechnology has emerged as one of the most important and frequently tested areas in UPSC General Science in recent years. Topics such as recombinant DNA technology, genetic engineering, genetically modified organisms and crops, CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing technology, stem cell research, cloning, monoclonal antibodies, vaccines and the biotechnology regulatory framework in India are regularly featured in Civil Services Prelims questions. The applications of biotechnology in agriculture, medicine, industry and environmental management, including bioremediation, biofuels and biopesticides, are important areas where science directly connects with policy and governance, making them especially relevant for UPSC preparation. Space and Satellite Technology is one of the most exciting and rapidly evolving areas of UPSC General Science, with ISRO's ambitious missions generating a steady stream of examination-worthy current affairs. A thorough understanding of India's space programme history, from Aryabhata in 1975 to the historic Chandrayaan-3 lunar landing in 2023 and the Aditya-L1 solar mission, is essential for every UPSC aspirant. Topics such as types of satellites and their orbits, remote sensing and GIS applications, launch vehicles including PSLV, GSLV and LVM3, India's navigation system NavIC, and the commercial and strategic applications of space technology are regularly tested in Civil Services Prelims. The growing role of private players in India's space sector following the opening up of the space industry and the establishment of IN-SPACe is an important current affairs dimension of this topic. Defence Technology is another high-priority area in UPSC General Science, encompassing India's missile development programme under DRDO, including the Agni, Prithvi, BrahMos, Akash, Nag and Prahar missile systems, as well as naval technology, fighter aircraft programmes like Tejas, the development of indigenous defence equipment under the Atmanirbhar Bharat initiative, and India's nuclear doctrine and capabilities. Questions on hypersonic missiles, drone technology, electronic warfare, cyber security and space-based defence systems are increasingly appearing in UPSC Prelims reflecting the rapidly changing nature of modern warfare and national security. Nanotechnology, the science of manipulating matter at the scale of individual atoms and molecules, has wide-ranging applications in medicine, electronics, energy, materials science and environmental remediation and is an important emerging technology topic in UPSC General Science. Nano-drug delivery systems, nanosensors, carbon nanotubes, graphene and the potential risks and regulatory challenges of nanotechnology are topics that UPSC aspirants must be familiar with. Science in Everyday Life covers the scientific principles behind common phenomena and technologies encountered in daily life, including the working of microwave ovens, refrigerators, mobile phones, GPS devices, MRI and CT scan machines, LED lighting, solar panels and water purification systems. Understanding the science behind food processing, nutrition, common medicines and health supplements, household chemicals and environmental phenomena like rainbows, mirages and lightning also forms an important part of this section. 1Chandrayaan-3 successfully landed on the Moon's South Pole in:
A. 2021
B. 2022
C. 2023
D. 2024
Answer: C - 2023
Chandrayaan-3 successfully landed on the Moon's South Pole on 23 August 2023, making India the first country to land near the lunar South Pole and the fourth country overall to achieve a soft lunar landing (after USA, USSR and China). The Vikram lander and Pragyan rover operated for one lunar day (14 Earth days). India celebrated 23 August as National Space Day.
2mRNA vaccines (like COVID-19 vaccines) work by:
A. Injecting weakened virus
B. Teaching cells to produce a harmless viral protein
C. Using viral DNA directly
D. Injecting viral antibodies
Answer: B - Teaching cells to produce a harmless viral protein
mRNA (messenger RNA) vaccines contain genetic instructions that teach the body's cells to produce a harmless piece of the target pathogen (e.g., the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2). The immune system recognises this protein as foreign, mounts a response and develops memory cells. The mRNA is quickly broken down - it does not enter the cell nucleus or alter DNA.
3The unit of radioactivity is:
A. Roentgen
B. Gray
C. Becquerel
D. Sievert
Answer: C - Becquerel
The Becquerel (Bq) is the SI unit of radioactivity - one Becquerel equals one nuclear decay per second. The older unit is the Curie (Ci): 1 Ci = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq. The Gray (Gy) measures absorbed radiation dose; the Sievert (Sv) measures equivalent dose (biological effect); the Roentgen measures exposure.
4CRISPR-Cas9 technology is used for:
A. Satellite communication
B. Gene editing
C. Solar energy harvesting
D. Water purification
Answer: B - Gene editing
CRISPR-Cas9 (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats) is a revolutionary gene editing technology that allows precise modification of DNA sequences. It acts like molecular scissors. Developed by Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier (Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2020), it has applications in medicine (genetic disease treatment), agriculture (crop improvement) and research.
5The speed of sound is maximum in which medium?
A. Vacuum
B. Air
C. Water
D. Steel
Answer: D - Steel
Sound travels fastest in solids (e.g., steel: ~5,960 m/s), then liquids (water: ~1,480 m/s), then gases (air: ~343 m/s). Speed of sound = 0 in vacuum. Sound is a mechanical wave - the closer and more strongly bonded the molecules, the faster sound propagates. Temperature also affects speed: higher temperature → faster sound in gases.
6Nanotechnology involves working with materials at the scale of:
A. Micrometres (10⁻⁶ m)
B. Nanometres (10⁻⁹ m)
C. Picometres (10⁻¹² m)
D. Millimetres (10⁻³ m)
Answer: B - Nanometres (10⁻⁹ m)
Nanotechnology involves manipulating matter at the scale of 1-100 nanometres (10⁻⁹ metres). At this scale, materials exhibit unique physical and chemical properties. Applications include nano-medicines (targeted drug delivery), nano-composites (stronger lighter materials), nano-electronics, water purification membranes and sunscreens.
7Stem cells are important in medicine because:
A. They are found only in bone marrow
B. They can differentiate into various cell types
C. They fight infections directly
D. They are a type of blood cell
Answer: B - They can differentiate into various cell types
Stem cells are undifferentiated cells capable of self-renewal and differentiation into specialised cell types. Embryonic stem cells are pluripotent (can form any body cell). Adult stem cells are multipotent (limited range). Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells (iPSCs) are reprogrammed adult cells. Medical applications include treatment of blood disorders, spinal injuries and diabetes.
8The Agni-V missile is classified as:
A. Anti-aircraft missile
B. Intercontinental Ballistic Missile (ICBM)
C. Cruise missile
D. Anti-tank missile
Answer: B - Intercontinental Ballistic Missile (ICBM)
Agni-V is India's first Intercontinental Ballistic Missile (ICBM) with a range exceeding 5,000 km (some reports say 8,000 km). It can carry a nuclear warhead and brings most of Asia and parts of Europe within India's strike range. Developed by DRDO, it was successfully tested and inducted into service. India uses the "no first use" nuclear doctrine.
9Photovoltaic cells convert which energy into electricity?
A. Wind energy
B. Thermal energy
C. Solar (light) energy
D. Nuclear energy
Answer: C - Solar (light) energy
Photovoltaic (PV) cells convert solar light energy directly into electricity through the photovoltaic effect - when photons (light particles) knock electrons loose from atoms in a semiconductor material (usually silicon), generating an electric current. India has one of the world's largest solar power programmes under the National Solar Mission.
10The Human Genome Project was completed in:
A. 1990
B. 2003
C. 2010
D. 2015
Answer: B - 2003
The Human Genome Project (HGP) was an international scientific research project that sequenced and mapped all human genes (approximately 20,000-25,000 genes, 3 billion base pairs). Initiated in 1990, it was formally completed in April 2003. It has revolutionised medicine, enabling genetic testing, personalised medicine and understanding of genetic diseases.
