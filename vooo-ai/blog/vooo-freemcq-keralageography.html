🌴 Kerala Geography & Science
1The capital of Kerala is:
A. Kozhikode
B. Kochi
C. Thiruvananthapuram
D. Thrissur
Answer: C — Thiruvananthapuram
Thiruvananthapuram (Trivandrum) is the capital of Kerala. It is named after the snake deity Ananthashayana Vishnu (Thiru-Anantha-Puram = sacred city of Lord Anantha). It is home to the Padmanabhaswamy Temple, one of the wealthiest temples in the world, and the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC).
2Kerala is known as God's Own Country mainly because of:
A. Its temples
B. Its scenic natural beauty
C. Its high GDP
D. Its ancient history
Answer: B — Its scenic natural beauty
Kerala is branded as "God's Own Country" due to its extraordinary natural beauty — serene backwaters, lush hill stations, pristine beaches, dense forests and diverse wildlife. The slogan was coined by the Kerala Tourism Department and has become one of the most successful tourism campaigns in India.
3The Silent Valley National Park in Kerala is significant because:
A. It has no wildlife
B. It is an undisturbed tropical rainforest
C. It is India's largest national park
D. It is a desert ecosystem
Answer: B — It is an undisturbed tropical rainforest
Silent Valley National Park in Palakkad district is one of India's last undisturbed stretches of tropical rainforest. It was saved from a hydroelectric project after a major environmental campaign in the 1970s-80s. The park is part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve and is home to the endangered lion-tailed macaque.
4ISRO's Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC) is located in:
A. Bengaluru
B. Chennai
C. Thiruvananthapuram
D. Hyderabad
Answer: C — Thiruvananthapuram
The Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC) in Thiruvananthapuram is ISRO's lead centre for launch vehicle technology. It develops the PSLV and GSLV rockets. Named after Vikram Sarabhai, the Father of India's Space Programme, it is one of the most important space research centres in Asia.
5Kerala's backwaters are a network of:
A. Rivers only
B. Interconnected canals, lakes and lagoons
C. Artificial reservoirs
D. Underground water channels
Answer: B — Interconnected canals, lakes and lagoons
Kerala's backwaters are a network of interconnected canals, rivers, lakes and inlets formed by the action of waves and longshore drift. Vembanad Lake (India's longest lake) and Ashtamudi Lake are the most important. The Nehru Trophy Boat Race is held on Punnamada Lake, Alappuzha every year.
6Kerala was the first state in India to achieve:
A. Highest GDP
B. 100% literacy
C. Complete electrification
D. Zero poverty
Answer: B — 100% literacy
Kerala was the first state in India to achieve near-total literacy (100% functional literacy). The Kottayam district was the first district in India to achieve 100% literacy in 1989. Kerala's literacy rate of about 96% is the highest among Indian states, attributed to the early spread of education by princely states and the Church.
7The Periyar River is the longest river in Kerala. It originates in:
A. Nilgiri Hills
B. Anaimalai Hills
C. Cardamom Hills
D. Palakkad Gap
Answer: B — Anaimalai Hills
The Periyar River (244 km) is the longest river in Kerala, originating in the Anaimalai Hills (Sivagiri peak) of the Western Ghats. The Idukki Dam and Cheruthoni Dam are built across it. The Periyar National Park and Tiger Reserve in Thekkady surrounds the Periyar Lake created by the Mullaperiyar Dam.
8Kathakali is a classical dance-drama of Kerala. It depicts stories from:
A. Greek mythology
B. Hindu epics Ramayana and Mahabharata
C. Buddhist texts
D. Mughal history
Answer: B — Hindu epics Ramayana and Mahabharata
Kathakali is a highly stylised classical Indian dance-drama form from Kerala, depicting stories from the Hindu epics Ramayana and Mahabharata, and the Puranas. It is known for its elaborate costumes, colourful make-up (chutti), face masks and headdresses. The performances last all night and require years of training.
9The Mattancherry Palace in Kochi was built by:
A. The Portuguese
B. The Dutch
C. The British
D. The Zamorins
Answer: A — The Portuguese
The Mattancherry Palace (Dutch Palace) in Kochi was built by the Portuguese around 1555 CE and presented to the Cochin Raja Veera Kerala Varma. It was later renovated by the Dutch in 1663, hence the name Dutch Palace. It is famous for its exquisite murals depicting scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata.
10Kerala's Spice Coast was famous for trade in which spice?
A. Turmeric
B. Cinnamon
C. Black pepper
D. Cardamom
Answer: C — Black pepper
Kerala's Malabar Coast was historically famous for black pepper, which was so valuable it was called Black Gold. The search for a direct sea route to the Spice Coast of Kerala drove Vasco da Gama to discover the sea route to India in 1498, landing at Kozhikode (Calicut). Kerala still produces about 97% of India's black pepper.
