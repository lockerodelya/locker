🌺 Puducherry History & Culture
1Puducherry was under the colonial rule of which country?
A. Britain
B. Portugal
C. France
D. Netherlands
Answer: C — France
Puducherry (formerly Pondicherry) was a French colonial territory for about 280 years. The French East India Company established its headquarters here in 1674. The French settlement peacefully merged with India on 1 November 1954, though the formal transfer of power was completed in 1962. French influence is still visible in architecture, cuisine and language.
2Auroville, the international township near Puducherry, was founded by:
A. Mahatma Gandhi
B. The Mother (Mirra Alfassa)
C. Sri Aurobindo
D. Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: B — The Mother (Mirra Alfassa)
Auroville was founded on 28 February 1968 by the Mother (Mirra Alfassa), the spiritual collaborator of Sri Aurobindo. Endorsed by UNESCO, it is a universal township meant for human unity beyond nationality, religion and politics. The Matrimandir (Soul of Auroville) is a golden spherical meditation centre at its heart. About 3,000 people from 50+ countries live there.
3Sri Aurobindo, the great philosopher-yogi, lived in Puducherry from:
A. 1910 to 1950
B. 1920 to 1960
C. 1900 to 1940
D. 1930 to 1960
Answer: A — 1910 to 1950
Sri Aurobindo (1872-1950) arrived in Pondicherry in 1910 and lived there until his death in 1950. He developed Integral Yoga — a spiritual path combining karma yoga, bhakti yoga and jnana yoga. The Sri Aurobindo Ashram, founded in 1926, continues to be a major spiritual centre attracting seekers from around the world.
4The French Quarter (White Town) of Puducherry is known for:
A. Ancient Hindu temples
B. French colonial architecture with tree-lined boulevards
C. Mughal architecture
D. Buddhist monasteries
Answer: B — French colonial architecture with tree-lined boulevards
The French Quarter (White Town) of Puducherry features well-preserved French colonial architecture — mustard-yellow buildings, tree-lined boulevards, a seafront promenade (Boulevard) and French street names. The area retains its distinctive French character with French restaurants, cafes and the French consulate, earning Puducherry the nickname "The French Riviera of the East."
5Puducherry is a Union Territory with a legislature. Its official languages include:
A. Tamil only
B. Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam and French
C. Tamil and French only
D. Tamil, French and Hindi
Answer: B — Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam and French
Puducherry has four regions: Puducherry (Tamil-speaking), Karaikal (Tamil-speaking), Mahe (Malayalam-speaking) and Yanam (Telugu-speaking) — each an enclave within different states. French is an associate official language, reflecting the colonial heritage. This makes Puducherry the only Indian UT with four geographically separated regions.
6The Basilica of the Sacred Heart of Jesus in Puducherry is significant because:
A. It is India's oldest church
B. It is a beautiful French Gothic-style church
C. It was built by Akbar
D. It has the largest congregation
Answer: B — It is a beautiful French Gothic-style church
The Basilica of the Sacred Heart of Jesus in Puducherry is a stunning French Gothic-style church built in 1907. With its twin towers, stained glass windows depicting the life of St. Joan of Arc and the Sacred Heart, it is one of the most beautiful churches in South India. The Church of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception (1791) is another landmark.
7Bharathi (Subramania Bharati), the great Tamil poet, lived in Puducherry to escape:
A. Poverty
B. British arrest warrants for his revolutionary writings
C. Religious persecution
D. A personal dispute
Answer: B — British arrest warrants for his revolutionary writings
Subramania Bharati (Mahakavi Bharathiyar), the revolutionary Tamil poet, lived in Pondicherry from 1908-1918 to escape British arrest warrants issued for his revolutionary and nationalistic writings in Tamil journals. He was a fierce advocate of independence, women's rights and social equality. His songs and poems remain deeply influential in Tamil culture.
8Puducherry's economy is significantly based on:
A. Heavy industry
B. Tourism, textiles and IT
C. Agriculture only
D. Fishing only
Answer: B — Tourism, textiles and IT
Puducherry's economy is based on tourism (French heritage, beaches, Auroville), textiles (especially handloom and khadi), IT/ITES services, fishing, rice mills and small industries. The SIPCOT industrial estate has attracted investments. Puducherry has high per capita income due to its small size and diverse economic activities.
9The Chunnambar Backwaters in Puducherry are known for:
A. Crocodile sanctuary
B. Boat rides and mangrove ecosystems
C. Saltwater fishing only
D. Industrial port
Answer: B — Boat rides and mangrove ecosystems
Chunnambar (Chunnambar Boat House) is a scenic backwater resort about 8 km from Puducherry town, popular for boat rides, water sports and picnics. The Chunnambar River estuary has mangrove forests. Boat rides connect to Paradise Beach (Plage Paradiso), accessible only by boat, making it one of the most pristine beaches in South India.
10Puducherry merged with India on:
A. 15 August 1947
B. 1 November 1954
C. 26 January 1950
D. 2 October 1960
Answer: B — 1 November 1954
Puducherry was peacefully handed over to India on 1 November 1954 when the elected Municipal Council voted to merge with India and the French administration transferred de facto control. However, the formal legal transfer (Treaty of Cession) was signed on 28 May 1956 and ratified by French Parliament in 1962. It became a Union Territory on 1 July 1963.
